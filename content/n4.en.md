# How to start running and keep going when life gets busy

Start with shorter runs than you think you can manage, plan them around your real week, and expect some weeks not to work out. What matters is whether you come back, not whether you never miss.

## Why new runners stop after a few weeks

Most people start too hard. Six times a week, long routes from day one. The first week goes fine. Then a meeting runs late, you catch a cold or there's a weekend at your parents', one run drops out, then another. A month later it's a break that's hard to come back from.

## Five steps to keep running

### 1. Give the goal a date

"Start running" is a wish. "Run 5 km by the end of December" is a goal. The date tells you how much time you have and how fast to build up.

### 2. Start slower than you want to

Mix walking and running and build up gradually. A coach or a beginner plan will give you the exact training. This is about sticking with it.

### 3. Put the runs in your calendar

Not "sometime this week" but a specific day and time that really exists in your week. If Tuesdays always run long, don't plan for Tuesday.

### 4. When you're short on time, shorten, don't cancel

Fifteen minutes of mobility instead of an hour's run. Three kilometres instead of ten. A short session keeps the rhythm; a cancelled one breaks it.

### 5. After a break, come back with an easier run

Don't pick up at the pace where you stopped, and don't make up for missed kilometres. Start with an easier run and build again. More in [What to do when your plan falls apart](/en/when-your-plan-falls-apart).
