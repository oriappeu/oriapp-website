# What to do when your plan falls apart

When a bad week breaks your plan, don't try to catch up. Shrink the next step, pick up where you are, and recalculate the goal. The plan didn't fall apart because you're weak. It assumed you'd be the same every week.

## Why plans fall apart

Plans usually get made on a Sunday evening, when you have energy and a clear head. Then Wednesday comes. A meeting runs late, a project catches fire, someone at home gets sick. The plan assumes a version of you with the same time and energy every day. That version doesn't exist.

A missed day doesn't break anything on its own. What comes after it does. The task moves to tomorrow, tomorrow there are two, a week later there's a pile. And nobody wants to come back to a pile.

## Six steps back to your goal

### 1. Don't treat it as failure

A missed week is information, not a verdict. It tells you how much you can really manage right now. Nothing more.

### 2. Don't try to catch up

Don't try to cram the missed tasks into the next few days. Let them go. Catching up is a common reason a second attempt ends faster than the first.

### 3. Shrink the next step

Not "tomorrow I'll do the whole thing", but the smallest version that still counts. [Ten minutes of vocabulary](/en/stay-consistent-learning-a-language) instead of an hour of English. [Fifteen minutes of stretching](/en/start-running-and-keep-going) instead of a long run. A small step that gets done restarts a habit better than a big one you keep putting off.

### 4. Pick the activity by energy, not by plan

When you're drained, the same task on a different day won't help. A different task will. Deep work needs strength; a small thing needs a moment.

### 5. Pick up where you are

Don't plan a comeback "from Monday". Start today, with a smaller step, from wherever you are. The goal stays the same; the route to it changes.

### 6. Recalculate the goal, not yourself

When the delay adds up, move a milestone or cut the scope. A plan is a tool. It should work for you, not the other way round.

## When it isn't about the plan

If things haven't been working for a long time and you feel drained by everything, it probably isn't about the plan. Talk to someone close to you or to a professional. Ori isn't a replacement for therapy or medical care.
