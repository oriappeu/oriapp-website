# How to stay consistent learning a language when you're tired after work

Don't rely on willpower. Set a small daily minimum you can manage even on a bad day, tie it to something you already do, and treat the exam date as a milestone, not a whip.

## Why it doesn't happen after work

After nine hours at work, it isn't the studying that drains you. It's deciding where to start. Grammar, listening, vocabulary or a podcast? By the time you've decided, it's 10 pm and you're on Instagram.

## Five steps to stick with a language

### 1. Break the goal into milestones

"Learn English" can't be finished. "Pass B2 in June" can. Break it into a few milestones, such as basics, B1 grammar, speaking with a tutor, and the exam. Each milestone gets its own month.

### 2. Set a daily minimum

Ten minutes of vocabulary counts. Your minimum is what you can manage after your worst day. Anything extra is a bonus, not a duty.

### 3. Have two modes: full and small

Full mode might be 45 minutes of listening and grammar. Small mode is 10 minutes of vocabulary. Choose by energy, not by guilt.

### 4. Tie studying to something you already do

An English podcast before bed. Vocabulary with your morning coffee. When studying hangs on a habit, you don't have to decide to do it every time.

### 5. When a week drops out, don't catch up

Don't cram the missed lessons into the weekend. Pick up with a small step from where you are. More in [What to do when your plan falls apart](/en/when-your-plan-falls-apart).
