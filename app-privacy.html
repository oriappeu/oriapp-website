Privacy Policy — the Ori app

Ori · test version · last updated 15 October 2026

Ori is a self-development app that turns a goal into a roadmap and daily sessions, and plans them around your day. This page says what data the app keeps, why, who else handles it, and how to get it removed. It is written for the closed test phase; it will be revised before a public launch. The website's waitlist has its own page. Pupils in a school pilot also get a one-screen summary in the app, “Ori's rules for you”.

Who is responsible

Until our company is registered, Ori is run by its two founders, Damian Knoth and Jindřich Novák, Czech Republic, who decide together how your data is used (joint controllers). Either of us can handle your request: oriapp.eu@gmail.com. When a company takes over this role, we will tell you before it happens.

Who can use Ori

Ori is for people aged 16 and over; in the Czech Republic, from 15. Pupils in a school pilot run together with Ori join with a pilot code, give their own consent in the app before using Ori's AI, and confirm that a parent or guardian knows and agrees. For everyone under 18, Ori applies stricter rules, for example more careful plans and stricter safety checks; reminders to pupils in a pilot never name what they are working on. Please give your real birth year; Ori uses it for these protections. If no birth year is known, Ori treats the account as under 18.

What we store

  • Account: your email address and a hashed password, handled by Supabase Auth. We never see your password. If you sign in with Google, Ori receives only your name, email address and profile picture. You can also add a name and a photo, and you give your birth year (for the under-18 protections above and to plan your energy over the day) and confirm your age. We also keep when you signed up and last used Ori, which setup steps you finished, and the state of your test access.
  • Your content: the goals, milestones, sessions, habits, tasks and feedback you enter in the app, and the challenges you join. Streaks and Life Balance are worked out from your records each time; they are not stored on their own.
  • Your day: working hours and breaks, your chronotype, the time windows and standing times you set, blocks you add (for example a school timetable) with their notes, away periods, places and travel times, what you decide about calendar meetings, sessions you pin or move and days you change, the energy level you tap, and which sessions, habits and tasks you finish.
  • What Ori learns from: the energy levels you tap, the blocks and sessions you move, and how long your sessions really take shape what Ori suggests next, for example where it places a session and how long it plans it. This is how Ori plans, so there is no switch for it. You can see what Ori holds in Profile → See everything Ori holds.
  • Health information: goals, messages, notes and feedback can contain health information, for example about sleep, food, exercise, pain or how you feel. Ori uses it only with your explicit consent; see Safety checks and Why we may use your data.
  • Session feedback, read to improve Ori: after a session you can rate it, say how much pain you felt during it (asked on physical sessions; it sets how much load the next sessions put on that area) and write a note. The founders read these answers and notes, including those of users under 18, to find what goes wrong with sessions (unclear steps, wrong time estimates, unsuitable resources) and to improve how Ori writes them; an AI assistant (Anthropic's Claude) helps us read them. Before we read them, your account is replaced by a random code, and email addresses, phone numbers and links are removed from the text. That working copy stays on our own computer and is deleted after each review; what we keep from a review describes problems in general words, never your notes. Deleting a goal deletes its notes; deleting your account deletes all of them.
  • What Ori remembers: for each goal, a short summary (at most 800 characters) of what you told Ori when setting it up — why it matters to you, your constraints and your level. It is shown to you on the goal's Roadmap, where you can edit or delete it, and it is sent with that goal's AI requests.
  • AI usage log: for each AI request, which feature made it, the model used, token counts, cost, timing, whether it succeeded, and the device identifier below. We use it to see what Ori's AI costs per account and per device. The log does not contain the text of your goals or answers. Calls made by the safety check are logged without your account.
  • Device identifier: a random code made when Ori is first opened on a device. It is sent with app-usage events and AI requests, so we can tell your devices apart. It says nothing about you, and it stays on the device when you sign out.
  • App usage: which screens you open, which setup steps and in-app guidance you finish, which notices Ori shows you and how you answer them, when you open the app and when you start or finish a session — event names and counts, never the content of your goals or messages, and never which safety screens you saw. To follow how the test is going, the founders see per account the email address, goal titles and these counts.
  • Problem reports (only if you send one): what you write, the email and name on your account, a screenshot if you add one, your device and browser type, the last screens you opened and the app's recent error messages. Never the text of your goals or messages. The founders read reports, with the help of an AI assistant (Anthropic's Claude). Copies we download for a review are deleted when it ends, and our review notes don't carry your name or email.
  • Notifications (only if you allow them): your device's push address, its platform and app version, and which reminders were planned, sent and opened. Besides reminders for your own sessions and habits, adults get at most three short “welcome back” notes after 3, 7 and about 14 days away, each naming their own next step; you can turn them off with “Welcome back after a few days” in Profile → Notifications.
  • A reason for leaving: if you delete your account, you can tell us why. That answer is kept without any link to you.
  • Google Calendar (only if you connect it): see Google user data below.

Safety checks

Goals and messages can touch on health, for example weight, food, sleep, exercise, alcohol or how you feel. To check plans for risks, a separate automatic safety check reads every message you write to Ori's AI and every AI reply before you see it. It looks for things like a crisis, an unsafe diet or training plan, or harm to someone else. When it finds one, Ori shows a fixed screen with help lines instead of the AI reply, makes the plan gentler, or pauses part of a goal. Nobody is locked out of the app. The checks can miss things, and they can also flag things by mistake.

  • What the check reads: your message, the last 10 turns of that conversation, the goal and its “why”, and your age group (adult or under 18).
  • What is kept: for up to 48 hours after the last safety signal, short notes such as “support mode on”, stored under a coded key without your name or email, so that the same support applies on every device; they are deleted automatically after that. On the goal, which step of gentle support Ori is showing and whether you tapped “My doctor said I can start”; this is deleted with the goal. For 30 days, a short review note so we can check that the safety check works: the area, the result, a short quote with names and numbers removed, your age group and the day. It has no name or email, but it could be linked back to you, so we treat it as your personal data.
  • Who is told what: your school and your parents can't see what you write in Ori, and nobody watches Ori in real time. A small team reviews short safety notes without names, to check that the safety features work. If a note shows that someone may be in serious danger from a crime, for example a plan to hurt a specific person or the abuse of a child, Czech law can require us to inform the police. The police then decide what happens next. Thoughts of self-harm on their own are not reported to anyone; Ori shows support lines instead.
  • If a check got it wrong, tap “You got this wrong”. You can add one line. A stronger AI checks the decision once more; some decisions can't be changed this way, for example under the stricter rules for under-18s. We don't keep your line. Every dispute is looked at in our weekly review.
  • Text that suggests you are under 18 switches on the stricter rules for up to 48 hours. It is not saved in your profile.
  • If you or someone else is in danger, call 112 (155 for medical help); children and young people can call Linka bezpečí 116 111.

On your device

Ori keeps data in your browser's or phone's storage so the app works: your sign-in session, and a copy of your goals, sessions and settings so the app is fast and works offline. These are needed for the service you asked for, so we don't ask for consent for them. Signing out removes them from this device; only the device identifier and a few technical switches stay. If you are signed out from another device (for example with “Sign out everywhere”), this device keeps its copy locked until you sign in again, so changes it had not sent yet are not lost; if someone else signs in on it, the copy is deleted first.

  • Check-in reminder (only if you turn it on): if, on a support screen, you tap “Check in with me next time”, Ori saves a small reminder only on this device for up to 3 days. It has no words in it, and it is never sent to Ori or anyone else. It is removed when you sign out.
  • Your region for emergency numbers: to show the right emergency and help numbers, Ori reads your phone's time zone on your phone. The result is not sent to Ori or stored.

Who processes it for us

These companies handle data only on our instructions, under data-processing terms:

  • Supabase stores the database and files and handles sign-in, in the EU (Frankfurt).
  • Anthropic (USA) runs Ori's AI. It receives the text of your goals and their summary, your answers in goal setup, your messages when you discuss or change a goal, your session feedback (including the pain rating), today's energy level, how many of today's habits and tasks you finished, your next milestone and session, how long your sessions usually take, your streak (adults only), whether you are under 18, and the one line you may add when you tap “You got this wrong”. It uses them to write your plans and to run the safety check. It does not receive your email address, your name or your birth year, and nothing received from Google Calendar is sent to it. Anthropic does not use this data to train its models. It deletes it within 30 days, but may keep content that its own safety systems flag for up to 2 years, and the classification scores for up to 7 years. We also use Anthropic's Claude as an assistant when we read problem reports and session feedback, as described above.
  • Render runs our server, in the EU (Frankfurt), and Vercel (USA) delivers the web app. Both handle your requests, including your IP address, and keep short-lived logs of them for the time their service sets.
  • Resend (USA) sends Ori's emails to you, for example a warning before an unused account is deleted. It receives your email address and the email text.
  • Google Firebase Cloud Messaging and your browser's push service (from Google, Apple or Mozilla) deliver notifications. They receive your device's push address and the notification text, which can name your session, goal or habit. Pupils in a school pilot get only general texts.

Google is not our processor: if you sign in with Google or connect Google Calendar, Google gives us the data described in Google user data under its own terms, and your Google profile picture is loaded from Google's servers. The YouTube Data API receives only search keywords for session resources, never anything about you.

Transfers outside the EU

Anthropic, Vercel, Resend and Google process data in the USA. Vercel, Resend, Render and Google are certified under the EU–US Data Privacy Framework (European Commission decision of 10 July 2023). Our contract with Anthropic uses the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses (2021). Supabase and Render keep your data in the EU; as companies based outside the EU, they may reach it for support under the same safeguards. You can ask us for a copy of the safeguards.

We do not sell data or show ads. We share your data only with the providers listed above, and with public authorities such as the police only where Czech or EU law requires it.

Google user data

Connecting Google Calendar is optional. When you connect it, Ori asks Google for three permissions and uses each for one thing only:

  • See the list of your calendars (calendar.calendarlist.readonly) — so you can choose which calendars Ori should respect.
  • View events on your calendars (calendar.events.readonly) — Ori reads the times of events on the calendars you chose, so it never plans a session over something you already have. Event titles are shown only to you, in your own schedule; in the history Ori keeps of past days, an event is recorded only as a busy time, without its title.
  • Create and change events on calendars you own (calendar.events.owned) — while your calendar is connected, Ori adds your planned sessions to it as events titled “Ori session”, without the name of your goal or any description of it, so that a shared calendar shows nothing about your goals. Ori updates or removes those events when your plan changes. Ori never changes or deletes an event it did not create.

To stay connected, Ori stores the access token Google issues, and which calendars you chose, in its database. Calendar data is not sold, not used for advertising, not used to train AI models, not sent to AI services, and not shared with anyone.

Ori's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

You can disconnect Google Calendar in the app at any time (Profile → Integrations). Disconnecting revokes Ori's access at Google and deletes the stored token; you can choose to remove upcoming “Ori session” events at the same time. You can also revoke access at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

How we protect your data

This applies to everything Ori stores, and above all to the most sensitive part: what comes from your Google account (the times of your calendar events and the access token Google issues).

  • Encryption in transit: everything between the app, Ori's server and our providers travels over HTTPS (TLS).
  • Encryption at rest: the database and the file storage are run by Supabase (EU region), which encrypts stored data.
  • Each account sees only its own data: the database itself, not only the app, allows every account to read and change only its own rows (row-level security).
  • Google tokens stay on the server: your Google Calendar access token is kept in a table that no app or browser can read; only Ori's server can. Secret keys live only on the server, never in the app. When you disconnect Google Calendar or delete your account, the token is deleted and Ori's access is revoked at Google.
  • Calendar data is kept to a minimum: Ori reads event times only to plan around them. Event titles are shown only to you, the history of past days keeps only busy times, and calendar data is never sent to AI services.
  • Passwords: stored only as hashes by Supabase Auth; Ori never sees them.
  • Sensitive actions need a fresh sign-in: deleting your account and changing your password.
  • Downloads: your data export is kept in private storage and handed to you through a link that expires.
  • Safety notes are kept apart: they carry no name or email and are stored under coded keys; only Ori's server can read them, and the screen where the founders review them needs a second sign-in factor.
  • Restricted access: only the people who operate Ori can reach the production database and the server settings.

No method of transmission or storage is completely secure. If a personal data breach is likely to put your rights at risk, Ori will tell you and the competent authority as the law requires.

Why we may use your data

  • Running your account and the app (your plans, calendar sync, reminders): our contract with you (GDPR Art. 6(1)(b)).
  • Health information in your goals, messages and feedback, the safety checks, and reading session feedback to improve Ori: your explicit consent (Art. 9(2)(a)), which you give in the app before using Ori's AI. You can withdraw it at any time; then the AI features stop, and the rest of the app keeps working. Pupils in a school pilot give this consent themselves and confirm that a parent or guardian knows and agrees.
  • Help in a crisis: if you write something that suggests someone may be in danger, Ori shows help lines even before you have agreed, and keeps the review note described above, to protect life and health (Art. 6(1)(d) and 9(2)(c)).
  • Keeping proof of your consent, and reporting a crime where the law requires it: our legal duty (Art. 6(1)(c)).
  • App-usage events, the AI usage log, problem reports and the founders' overview of the test: our legitimate interest in keeping Ori working and making it better (Art. 6(1)(f)). You can object to this; write to us.

Automatic decisions

The safety check decides automatically whether to show help lines instead of an AI reply, make a plan gentler, pause part of a goal, or switch on the stricter rules for under-18s for up to 48 hours. You are never locked out of the app. You can ask for a decision to be checked again (“You got this wrong”), and every dispute is read by a person in our weekly review. Ori does not use your data for advertising or to make any other decision about you.

Retention and deletion

  • Your account and content: as long as your account exists. If you don't use Ori for 12 months, we email you; if you still don't come back, the account is deleted after 13 months, and at least 30 days after that email.
  • Your day's records (what you ticked off on a day, energy taps): 90 days. A task or session you finished stays marked done for as long as it exists. Away periods: one year after they end.
  • Planning details: what you decided about a calendar meeting, 90 days after you last changed it; Ori's memory of where you moved blocks, 60 days; a changed day or your answer about a missed session, 30 days.
  • Deleted items: when you delete something, Ori keeps a small marker with only its identifier and the time, so another of your devices can't bring it back. It holds no title or text and is deleted with your account.
  • App-usage events and the AI usage log: 12 months. When you delete your account, your app-usage events are deleted; the AI usage log is kept for the rest of its 12 months without your account, with only the device identifier, so we can still see what Ori's AI costs.
  • Problem reports: 12 months after they are closed; screenshots 30 days after closing, and never longer than 90 days.
  • Notification addresses: removed 30 days after the device was last seen, and when you sign out on that device.
  • Your data export: the file is deleted 24 hours after it is made.
  • Safety records: short safety notes, 48 hours after the last safety signal; safety review notes, 30 days; gentle-support and doctor-tap records on a goal, until the goal is deleted.
  • Proof of your consent (what we showed you, the version and the time): as long as your account exists, plus 3 years.
  • Pupils who leave a school pilot: their Ori data is deleted within 14 days.
  • At Anthropic: 30 days; content its safety systems flag, up to 2 years; classification scores, up to 7 years.
  • Copies of the database: Ori does not run automatic backups yet. Before we change how data is stored, we take a full copy of the database. It is kept encrypted on the founders' computer and deleted after 30 days, so a deleted account can remain in such a copy for up to 30 days.
  • Some records are kept longer only if the law requires it, for example during a legal case.

During the test phase the database may be reset between rounds: every tester's account is then deleted in the way described below, and testers are told beforehand.

You can delete your account yourself, in the app: Profile → Privacy & Security → Delete my account. Deleting removes your account and its content, revokes Ori's access to your Google Calendar and removes Ori's upcoming “Ori session” events from it. A few things stay, without your name or email: the AI usage log (above), the proof of your consent (3 years), the reason for leaving if you gave one, and, if you were an invited tester, a scrambled (hashed) copy of your email address for 3 years, so the address does not get test access again on its own. If you cannot sign in, email oriapp.eu@gmail.com from the address you signed up with; deletion is then done within 14 days.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask for a copy of your data (including the safety records linked to your account and the proof of your consent), a correction, deletion, restriction or a portable copy, and you can object to processing based on our legitimate interest. You can download a copy yourself in the app (Profile → Privacy & Security → Export my data), or use the email above. You can also delete single things yourself: a problem report you sent (Profile → Report a problem → My reports), an energy reading or a place you named (Profile → See everything Ori holds), the note on a finished session, and everything Ori remembers from setting up your goals, at once (Clear AI memory). You can withdraw consent at any time in Profile → Privacy & Security; this does not affect what was done before. In the app the AI stops straight away; by email, within one working day.

Requests from a parent about a pupil's data: we tell the pupil first; we tell the parent which kinds of data we hold; we give the content only with the pupil's agreement or where the law requires it.

If you think we have handled your data wrongly, please tell us first; we will look into it and put it right. You can also complain to the Czech Office for Personal Data Protection (Úřad pro ochranu osobních údajů), uoou.gov.cz.
